**** Interstate Natural Gas Pipeline Efficiency

https://ingaa.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/10/10929.pdf

*Pipeline System Design*
- The initial design normally is based on peak day contractual
commitments plus an accommodation for future demand that can be reliably forecast

*J-Curves* - compares the delivered cost of fuel to the cost of pipe
- To determine the optimum combination of pipeline diameter and horsepower requirements

*Optimal Compressor Spacing*
- To determine optimal compressor spacing the trade off between delivered transportations cost for natural gas v.s. pipe mileage

*Transportation Efficiency of the pipeline system* - $\eta_{sys}$
- a combined product of the pipeline hydraulic efficiency $\eta_{pipeline}$
  - which measures losses between compressor units and the compressor unit efficiency $\eta_{stations}$
    - which includes both driver efficiency and compressor efficiency
- higher pressure loss equates to more actual work
  - which lowers the transportation efficiency
- compressor stations boost the pressure of gas to compensate for pressure losses along the line
  /pressure loss is related to friction, the length, and diameter of the pipe and the individual pressure losses due to obstructions such as bends, valves, or flow meters/
  - When designing its system, a pipeline company tries to optimize hydraulic efficiency
    - through pipeline routing, diameter and operating pressure selections, and unit efficiency
      - through its compressor unit selections
        - (including the engines, turbines, or electric motors that power the compressors).


*Compressor Selection*
- After determining the desired ratio between pipeline specifications and horsepower requirements
  - one can select compresor units that best meet its load profile and operating needs
    1. forecasted operating conditions
    2. unit's air emissions compliance
    3. installation costs
    4. projected operating costs
    5. projected maintenance costs
    6. unit's compatibiility with fleet of compressors
    7. overall efficiency of the compressor unit
       - combination of thermal efficiency of the prime mover and compression efficiency of the compressors themselves
    8. reliability of compressor unit components
    9. expertise of pipeline personnel with equipment

- Installation of one or more large units per compressor station versus several smaller units affects pipeline efficiency
  - to address variable market area customer demands, sometimes select smaller compressor units can be switched on and off to meet throughput and pressure needs
_Assuming the same configuration and location, two smaller compressor units will have a higher cost per horsepower compared to larger unit due to economies of scale_
- one fully-loaded, big unit uses fuel more efficiently and cost less than 2 small units
- in contrast, one fully-loaded small unit will be more fuel efficient and offer more flexibility than partially-loaded big unit
- similarly, operating mulitple small compressors achieves better fuel efficiency than a single large compressor
  - if the pipeline operates at less than max throughput (typically for a given a location)

*Pipeline Operations*
- Pipeline companies employ a number of techniques and procedures to maximize system efficiency
  - while satisfying the level of required customer flexibility and fulfilling contractual commitments
    1. flow simulation software for transient and real time modeling
       - for operations that rely on higher linepack
    2. shortening the outage time of high efficiency equipement
    3. seek approval for increase in MAOP through PHMSA
       - therefore reducing compressor fuel usage




*Conclusion*
/The greatest opportunity for maximizing both the economic and transportation efficiencies of a pipeline system is during the initial design and construction stage, when the optimum combination of pipe size, compression, and compressor unit components is chosen to meet projected demand/
