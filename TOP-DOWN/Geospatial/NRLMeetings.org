1. *EIA Form 923 (Generation and Fuel)*
   - While CEMS is hourly, EIA-923 provides the "ground truth" for fuel consumption and net generation at a monthly level. It is useful for cross-referencing CEMS data, which measures gross load.
     - *Net vs. Gross:*
       - Use the net_generation_mwh field to calculate the parasitic load of the plants, allowing you to tune your reV generation profiles to reflect what actually hits the grid.
     - *Heat Rates:*
       - You can derive operational heat rates ($MMBtu/MWh$) using the fuel consumption data. This helps validate the efficiency assumptions of the natural gas "supply" you are modeling.

2. *EIA Form 860 (Plant & Generator Inventory)*
   To associate your supply curve points with specific technologies, EIA-860 provides the metadata.
   - *Prime Movers:* Differentiate between "CA" (Combined Cycle Steam Part), "CT" (Combined Cycle Combustion Turbine), and "GT" (Gas Combustion Turbine).
   - *Operational Constraints:* Data on nameplate capacity and status (existing vs. retired) allows you to filter your validation set to only include modern, high-efficiency plants that reflect current natural gas potential.
